# Microphone types and phantom power

## Separate the transducer from its power supply
A microphone converts air-pressure changes into an electrical signal. A moving-coil dynamic microphone uses electromagnetic induction; a condenser capsule relies on changing capacitance and supporting electronics. These are operating principles, not rankings of artistic quality. The voice, room and placement can matter more than the label on the microphone. Passive ribbons require special care around powering and wiring; an active ribbon can have different requirements. Always check the particular microphone rather than treating every ribbon alike.

Phantom power supplies DC through a balanced microphone connection. It is not an audio effect and does not increase a microphone's acoustic sensitivity on command. Many studio condensers use a nominal 48 V supply, but permitted voltage and current vary. A USB microphone gets power through USB and usually does not need an interface's XLR phantom switch. Phantom powering and increasing preamp gain solve different problems.

## A practical connection routine
1. Identify the microphone's connection and documented power requirement.
2. Lower monitoring volume before changing cables or powering.
3. Connect suitable balanced wiring with phantom switched off.
4. Enable the required supply only for a compatible microphone and input.
5. Allow the equipment to settle, then set gain using the loudest intended performance.

A correctly wired balanced moving-coil microphone usually does not need phantom and tolerates it, but damaged cables, unbalanced adapters and some ribbon arrangements change that assessment. A safe routine avoids assuming that a connector alone guarantees compatibility.

## Worked troubleshooting example
Imagine a quiet condenser connected through an adapter to a laptop headphone socket. Turning a software fader upward cannot provide a missing compatible microphone input or phantom supply. First identify what that socket accepts. Then choose an appropriate signal path; do not buy extra gain merely because the track is quiet. Conversely, an already working dynamic microphone does not become a condenser when the 48 V switch is pressed.

## Common misconceptions and limits
A condenser is not automatically the best microphone for every room. Phantom power does not remove room reflections or repair a faulty lead. This guide intentionally gives no AT2020/AT2035 electrical specification table or ribbon-specific wiring instructions. Use the current manufacturer's requirements for exact hardware decisions. Distinguish low input level, missing power and poor placement before attempting processing.
