# Export formats, quality and delivery checks

## An export is a deliverable, not just a filename
Exporting creates media with chosen picture, sound and packaging settings. A useful decision begins with the destination: another editor, a long-term archive, a presentation device or an online service. A single convenient preset cannot promise the best trade-off for every destination. File extension, video codec and audio codec should be checked separately.

Bitrate describes the amount of encoded data per unit of time. With other choices held constant, allocating more data can improve the encoding result, but it does not restore missing source detail. The content and encoder matter: fine texture and rapid movement can stress settings that seemed adequate on a still talking-head test. Larger dimensions alone do not guarantee a better-looking delivery file.

## Original size estimate
Suppose an illustrative export averages 12 megabits per second for 90 seconds. The video payload is about 1,080 megabits, or roughly 135 megabytes using eight bits per byte and decimal units. Audio and packaging add more, and variable-bitrate output can differ from a simple target-rate estimate. The example is arithmetic, not a recommended platform setting.

## A delivery checklist
1. Confirm the intended duration and export range, including the actual ending.
2. Check dimensions, frame rate, codec/container compatibility and the intended color treatment.
3. Confirm that the correct audio mix, channels and sample rate are being delivered.
4. Export a representative test when settings or compatibility are uncertain.
5. Watch and listen to the resulting file, including the middle and the end, on a relevant player.

Inspect synchronization as well as picture quality. A correct-looking export dialog cannot prove that the file has sound or that audio remains aligned. Keep a suitable master and the project assets when additional revisions are expected instead of relying entirely on an upload copy.

## Misconceptions and scope
Changing an extension is not conversion, and recompressing an already damaged delivery file does not recover discarded detail. Two files with the same nominal bitrate can have different visual results. Exact social-platform limits, legal delivery compliance and every current codec option are outside this guide. Verify current destination requirements separately; do not fabricate a promised bitrate, loudness target or color-space rule when the requirement is unknown.
